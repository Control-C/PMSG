## 01 · Know Before You Fly

Your Shenzhen survival kit — start here before departure (and before the group chat reaches critical coffee mass).

### Passport, Visa & Arrival Card

Check visa requirements or visa-free eligibility for your nationality. Fill out the digital arrival card online if possible (via NIA 12367 mini-program in Alipay/WeChat or s.nia.gov.cn). Physical cards exist as backup.

Bring printed copies of: invitation letter (if any), hotel booking (with Chinese address), return tickets, and visa docs. Border officers like paper.

### Communication & Payments (Non-Negotiable)

**Download WeChat.** It is the superapp. You will need it for messaging, payments, transport, translation, and surviving.

Set up **Alipay** and **WeChat Pay** with an international card. Test them. Shenzhen is extremely cashless. Most places do not take foreign credit cards directly.

> **Pro tip from the group**: AlipayHK (Hong Kong number) can help with VPN + connectivity in one go.

### Connectivity & Apps

Google, YouTube, and many Western services are blocked. Download offline maps and translation tools before you fly.

**Must-have apps:**
- WeChat + Alipay
- DiDi (ride-hailing)
- AMap (navigation)
- Offline Google Translate / Microsoft Translator / Apple Maps (iOS works well)
- HelloChinese (optional but fun)

### Power

220V / 50Hz. EU/UK plugs and devices are fine. North American single-voltage tools may not be. Most outlets accept Type A (US-style 2-pin) and Type I (AU-style 3-pin).

## 02 · Arriving in Shenzhen

### From Shenzhen Bao'an Airport (SZX)

- **DiDi / Taxi**: Best with luggage or late arrival.
- **Metro Line 11**: Easy once you have Alipay Transit QR code set up. Scan in and out. Great first test of your payment setup.

### From Hong Kong

Multiple options (coach, ferry via SkyPier, high-speed rail, land border). Land crossings involve two immigration steps: exit HK, then enter Mainland. Do the digital arrival card in advance to skip kiosk queues.

**Octopus Card** (for time in Hong Kong): Physical cards need cash top-ups. iPhone users can get a digital “Octopus for Tourists” in Apple Wallet and top up with international cards.

## 04 · Explore the City

### Easy City Walks

- **Qianhai Citywalk**: Stone Park → Conference Center → Performing Arts Park → Happy Harbor (Bay Area Light Ferris wheel). ~2.5 hours.
- **Hongqiao Bridge**: 4 km elevated red pedestrian path through parks and hills.

### Coffee & Tea Scene (You Will Need This)

Shenzhen is packed with chains. Order via WeChat/Alipay mini-programs (scan QR, set sugar/ice, pick up by number). Translation features exist.

- **Luckin Coffee**: Try the Raw Coconut Latte (生椰拿铁).
- **Manner**: Better beans, bring your own cup for discount.
- **CHAGEE** and **HEYTEA**: Fresh fruit teas and milk teas. Go half sugar (半糖) or less.

## 05 · After the Faire: Hike, Surf, Recover

The faire is 12–13 December. The weekend before or the days after are perfect for outdoor recovery (or pre-game adventure). Shenzhen has real mountains and a surprisingly decent surf scene for a megacity.

### Hiking Highlights

**Wutong Mountain (梧桐山)** — Shenzhen’s highest peak at 943.7 m.  
Multiple trails of varying difficulty (North Road is more gradual, Lingyun and Xiutong are steeper, Dengyun is shadier). Expect stairs, subtropical forest, and on clear days views over the city, port, and toward Hong Kong. Free entry. Start early on weekends. Bring water and good shoes.

**Other solid options:**
- **Tanglang Mountain** — Easier than Wutong, good city views.
- **Xichong to Dongchong coastal trail** (Dapeng Peninsula) — Dramatic clifftop hike connecting two beaches. Full day, worth the travel.
- **Shenzhen Bay Park** — Flat, paved, easy greenway with Hong Kong views. Good for recovery day or cycling.
- **Lianhuashan Park** — Central, accessible, popular with locals.

### Surfing Spots

Shenzhen’s waves are usually modest (best with typhoon swell June–October), but there is a real local scene and beginner-friendly setups.

- **Dameisha Beach (大梅沙)**: Closest and easiest (Metro Line 8). Free, some shore break, lessons available. Crowded on weekends. Good for a quick post-faire dip or lesson.
- **Xiaomeisha (小梅沙)**: Nearby, paid entry, cleaner and less chaotic.
- **Xichong Beach (西涌)**: Best dedicated surf spot on the Dapeng Peninsula. Longer beach, proper surf clubs, beginner to intermediate waves when there’s swell. Worth the extra travel (DiDi or bus). Pair with the coastal hike.

**Maker note**: Some Seeeders are actual surfers. Watch for the PCB surfboard badge at the faire. Bring your board if you’re hardcore, or just rent.

**Practical**: Check conditions (Magicseaweed / local groups). Water is warm enough most of the year. Shark nets and lifeguards at main beaches.

## 07 · No Question is a Weird Question

- **Toilets**: Mix of sit and squat. Carry emergency toilet paper.
- **Water**: Don’t drink the tap. Buy bottles.
- **Power banks**: Need CCC logo or they may get confiscated.
- **Gifting**: Coffee, chocolate, local weirdness, and tinned fish are all accepted currencies.
- **Packing**: AirTag your passport. Print key addresses. Consider a clean phone or just bring yours (many people do fine). Bao’an Airport has free showers after customs if you need a reset.

Ask anything. The group has seen worse.
